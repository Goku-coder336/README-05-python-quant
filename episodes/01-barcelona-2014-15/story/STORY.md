# Gus and the moving goals

Run of the Champions, Episode 1: Barcelona 2014/15. An animated story film. 1920x1080, 30 fps, about 7 minutes.
Draft for the user's approval: nothing is recorded yet.

## The storyteller

**Gus**, an original character: an old groundsman with a flat cap, round glasses, a white moustache, a green
cardigan and a stick of chalk. He has marked the lines on football pitches all his life, and he tells this season
like a bedtime story, in the first person. He is fiction; everything he tells is fact, with a source.

- He opens and closes the film, and steps in between scenes to link the chapters.
- His mouth moves on the words of the voice track; he blinks, points, chalks, shrugs and celebrates.
- His chalkboard holds the numbers: goal tallies, results, and the analysis charts in chapter 6.
- Voice: ElevenLabs "George" (warm, older, British) on `eleven_multilingual_v2`, checked line by line.

## The look

- **Background**: a bold violet (`#4423b0` to `#5b31d1`), as you asked, following your reference clip.
- **The pitch**: white lines in perspective, seen from an angled camera that moves with the play: wide for the
  build-up, then a cut in close for the moment that matters (a shot, a save, a player beaten).
- **Players**: flat illustrated figures with posable limbs (run, dribble, kick, stretch, dive, celebrate).
  Plain kit colours only: Barcelona red with blue stripes, Juventus white with black stripes, keepers in yellow
  or orange. No faces, no badges, one stylised skin tone for everyone. Names on tags.
- **Goals from the final** are replays from StatsBomb event data (passes, carries, shots, saves, the defender
  beaten) and say "Reconstruction · positions illustrative" on screen. Close-ups say "positions and poses
  illustrative", because the data gives where things happened, not how bodies moved.
- **Other matches** (group, round of 16, quarter-final, semi-final) have no event data in StatsBomb's free set,
  so they are told as story scenes: Gus's chalkboard, scorelines, and the forwards acting out who scored, never
  a replay of a specific goal.
- Captions on every line, sources on every chart, British spelling, no em dashes.

### 1. Once upon a time

**1.3** He had three forwards. Lionel Messi. Neymar. Luis Suárez.

### 6. Gus's notebook

**6.1** Now, I keep a notebook. Here's what the data says about that final, according to StatsBomb.
*See:* Gus back at the chalkboard, opening a worn notebook. "All figures: StatsBomb open data".

**6.2** Expected goals, or xG, measures the quality of each chance. Barcelona finished on two point two seven. Juventus on one point three eight.
*See:* the xG lines climb across the board, goals marked. [SB]

**6.3** Barcelona made six hundred passes. Juventus made three hundred and seventy-seven.
*See:* passes per 15 minutes as chalk bars. [SB]

**6.4** Most of that gap came before half-time: three hundred and seventy-four to one hundred and seventy-five. After the break, two hundred and twenty-six to two hundred and two.
*See:* the first-half bars glow, then the second-half bars. [SB]

**6.5** But Juventus pressed more: one hundred and forty-four pressures to one hundred and twenty-two. Barcelona pressed higher: twenty-nine in the attacking third, to Juventus's eighteen.
*See:* Gus pushes white figures towards a red one on the board; the pressing-by-thirds chart. [SB]

**6.6** Barcelona's busiest passing link was Dani Alves to Rakitić: thirty-five completed passes before the first substitution.
*See:* a pass network on the pitch; the Alves to Rakitić line glows. [SB]

**6.7** And look at Messi. No Barcelona player appears in the data more: two hundred and ninety events, seventy-one of them passes.
*See:* Messi's bar against Dani Alves 280 and Jordi Alba 220. [SB]

**6.8** The man who scored eight in the group was all over the final. He just wasn't the one finishing the moves.
*See:* Messi's figure in the middle of a web of his passes; Gus nods slowly.
