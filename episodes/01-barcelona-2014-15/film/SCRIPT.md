# The case of the moving goals

Run of the Champions, Episode 1: Barcelona 2014/15. An animated short film in HyperFrames.
1920x1080, 30 fps, target 6:30 to 7:30.

## The idea

**Pip**, an original character: a red tactics-board magnet (the same glowing red disc as Barcelona's player
dots) with big eyes, thin arms and legs, and a magnifying glass. Pip lives on the tactics board and treats the
season as a case: Messi scored eight of Barcelona's fifteen group goals, then two of sixteen in the knockouts.
Where did the goals go? Pip follows them round by round: stacking goal blocks into piles, pinning results to the
board, moving the magnets to replay the goals of the final, and reading the numbers off the charts like evidence.

- Narration: ElevenLabs George (`eleven_multilingual_v2`), a calm analyst telling a mystery. Pip does not speak;
  Pip acts (reacts, points, carries, celebrates). One file per line; the timeline is built from the measured
  durations.
- Players are magnets on the board: Barcelona red with a glow, opponents grey. Only players who were on the pitch
  at that minute are named. Goal scenes come from StatsBomb event data and say
  "Reconstruction · positions illustrative".
- Captions for every line. British spelling, no em dashes on screen.
- Every number has a source tag (see `SOURCES.md`): [UEFA] official match pages and season statistics,
  [SB] StatsBomb open data for the final, [W] Wikipedia's 2014/15 Champions League page (group table), [ESPN],
  [AP], [RFI].

## Accuracy changes from the earlier version

- **Messi as top assister (6) is out**: UEFA says 6, ESPN says 4. The brief: leave out what sources disagree on.
- **Shot counts in the final are out** (StatsBomb 17 v 15, UEFA 18 v 14). xG stays, "according to StatsBomb".
- **"290 on-ball actions" becomes "290 events in the data, the most of any Barcelona player"**.
- **No claims about why.** The film shows what happened; the closing question is left to the viewer.
- **No player photos.** No faces or likenesses of real players: they are magnets.

## Script

Each line: narration, then what we see (Pip's action in **bold**), then the source.

### c0-open: The case

**0.1** In the group stage, Lionel Messi scored eight of Barcelona's fifteen goals.
*See:* a dark tactics board. Fifteen goal blocks drop into a row. **Pip pushes eight of them into a red pile labelled Messi, one by one, then dusts its hands.** "8 / 15".
*Source:* [UEFA]

**0.2** Then came the knockout rounds. Barcelona scored sixteen more. Messi scored two.
*See:* sixteen more blocks thud down. **Pip pushes two into Messi's pile, looks for more, finds none; its eyes go wide.** "2 / 16" lands with a low hit.
*Source:* [UEFA]

**0.3** Barcelona still won the Champions League. So where did the goals go?
*See:* **Pip lifts a magnifying glass to one eye; the lens fills the frame** and becomes the title card: "RUN OF THE CHAMPIONS", "Barcelona 2014/15", "The case of the moving goals".

**0.4** This is a case about where goals go. Follow them, and the season tells a different story.
*See:* the lens shrinks back into Pip's hand; **Pip tips an imaginary hat to camera.**

### c7-numbers: The evidence

**7.1** Now the evidence. The final in numbers, according to StatsBomb.
*See:* **Pip opens a case file**; "All figures: StatsBomb open data".

**7.2** Expected goals, or xG, measures the quality of each chance. The higher the number, the better the chances.
*See:* the xG axes draw; **Pip pulls the two lines across the chart like strings.**
*Source:* [SB]

**7.3** Barcelona finished on two point two seven. Juventus on one point three eight.
*See:* end labels "2.27" (red) and "1.38" (grey); goal markers on the lines. **Pip points at 2.27.**
*Source:* [SB]

**7.4** Here is every shot, sized by its xG.
*See:* shot map on the pitch, goals filled; **Pip hops between the big red circles near the goal.**
*Source:* [SB]

**7.5** Barcelona made six hundred passes. Juventus made three hundred and seventy-seven.
*See:* passes per 15 minutes, two series; Barcelona's first-half bars tallest. **Pip stacks the bars.**
*Source:* [SB]

**7.5b** In the first half, Barcelona made three hundred and seventy-four passes. Juventus made one hundred and seventy-five.
*See:* the first three bars of each side light up; **Pip stretches to the top of Barcelona's.**
*Source:* [SB]

**7.5c** After the break the gap closed: two hundred and twenty-six to two hundred and two.
*See:* the second-half bars light; the two sides almost level.
*Source:* [SB]

**7.6** But Juventus pressed more: one hundred and forty-four pressures to one hundred and twenty-two.
*See:* pressing by thirds: Juventus 60 / 66 / 18, Barcelona 45 / 48 / 29, units "pressures". **Pip is jostled by grey magnets.**
*Source:* [SB]

**7.6b** Barcelona pressed higher: twenty-nine pressures in the attacking third, to Juventus's eighteen.
*See:* the attacking-third column glows on both sides: 29 against 18.
*Source:* [SB]

**7.7** Barcelona's busiest passing link was Dani Alves to Rakitić: thirty-five completed passes before the first substitution.
*See:* pass network on the pitch (completed passes, to 77'): magnets at average positions, line width = passes; **Pip plucks the Alves to Rakitić line like a guitar string.**
*Source:* [SB]

**7.8** No Barcelona player appears in the data more than Messi: two hundred and ninety events, seventy-one of them passes.
*See:* Messi's card against the next two (Dani Alves 280, Jordi Alba 220). **Pip stands on Messi's bar.**
*Source:* [SB]

**7.9** And the player who set up the most shots was Rakitić, with four.

### c8-verdict: The verdict, and your call

**8.1** So, the whole run. Thirteen games. Eleven wins. Two defeats, both away from home.
*See:* the route complete; **Pip chalks the tally** 13, 11, 2.
*Source:* [UEFA]

**8.2** Thirty-one goals scored. Eleven conceded.
*See:* 31 and 11 land.
*Source:* [UEFA]

**8.2b** They won every home game, six out of six. Both defeats were by a single goal: three-two in Paris, three-two in Munich.
*See:* six home result cards glow red; the two 2-3 cards tilt in grey. **Pip nods.**
*Source:* [UEFA]

**8.3** In the group, Messi scored eight. In the knockouts, two. Neymar and Suárez scored twelve of the sixteen.
*See:* the opening blocks return; **Pip sweeps Neymar's and Suárez's knockout blocks together**: 12 of 16.
*Source:* [UEFA]

**8.4** Messi still finished joint top scorer, on ten goals, level with Neymar and Cristiano Ronaldo.
*See:* three piles of ten. **Pip nods slowly: case closed.**
*Source:* [UEFA] [ESPN] (both agree)

**8.4b** So, case closed? Not quite.
*See:* **Pip starts to close the case file, stops, and looks at the camera.**

**8.5** It was Barcelona's second treble, and they were the first European club to win the treble twice.
*See:* "2nd TREBLE"; "first club to win it twice · UEFA.com".
*Source:* [UEFA]

**8.6** Luis Enrique called it a transitional year. Ten years later, he won this competition again, with Paris Saint-Germain, in twenty twenty-five and twenty twenty-six.
