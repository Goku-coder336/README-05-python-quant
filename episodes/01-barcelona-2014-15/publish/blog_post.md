# Messi scored 8, then 2: how Barcelona won the 2014/15 Champions League

*Run of the Champions, Episode 1. Every Champions League winner's full run, from the group stage to the
final.*

[VIDEO: paste your YouTube link here. Most blog editors turn it into a player.]

In the 2014/15 group stage, Lionel Messi scored 8 of Barcelona's 15 goals. More than half.

Then the knockout rounds started. Barcelona scored 16 more goals. Messi scored two of them.

Barcelona still won the Champions League. They won the treble. So what happened to the goals?

[IMAGE: blog-images/1-messi-paradox.png]
*Each block is one Barcelona goal. Messi's are in gold.*

## A transitional year

It was Luis Enrique's first season in charge. After the final, he called it "a difficult year, a
transitional year". Keep that phrase in mind. It ages well.

Up front he had three forwards who could all score: Messi, Neymar and Luis Suárez. The interesting
question was never whether Barcelona would score. It was who would.

## Group F: the Messi show

Barcelona were drawn with Paris Saint-Germain, Ajax and APOEL.

- APOEL at home, 1-0. The scorer was Gerard Piqué.
- PSG away, lost 3-2. Messi and Neymar scored.
- Ajax at home, 3-1. Neymar, Messi and Sandro.
- Ajax away, 2-0. Both Messi.
- APOEL away, 4-0. A Messi hat-trick, plus Suárez.
- PSG at home, 3-1. Messi, Neymar and Suárez.

Barcelona won the group on 15 points, two ahead of PSG. Fifteen goals, eight of them Messi's. If you
had stopped watching there, you would have called it the Messi show.

## The knockouts: the goals move

**Round of 16, Manchester City.** Barcelona won 2-1 in Manchester, with both goals from Suárez, and 1-0
at home through Ivan Rakitić. 3-1 on aggregate, and no goal from Messi.

**Quarter-final, PSG again.** Barcelona won 3-1 in Paris (Neymar, and Suárez twice) and 2-0 at home
(Neymar twice). 5-1 on aggregate. After two knockout rounds the tally read Suárez 4, Neymar 3,
Rakitić 1, Messi 0.

**Semi-final, Bayern Munich.** This is where Messi did score: twice in the 3-0 first leg at home, with
Neymar adding the third. Barcelona lost 3-2 in Munich, both goals from Neymar. 5-3 on aggregate.

## The final in Berlin

6 June 2015, the Olympiastadion. Rakitić scored after four minutes. Álvaro Morata equalised ten minutes
into the second half. Suárez put Barcelona back in front in the 68th minute, and Neymar made it 3-1 deep
into stoppage time.

[IMAGE: blog-images/6-rakitic-reconstruction.png]
*Rakitić's opener, reconstructed from StatsBomb event data. Player positions are from the moment of the
shot.*

According to StatsBomb, Barcelona had 17 shots worth 2.27 expected goals (xG), and Juventus had 15 worth
1.38. (UEFA's official count is 18 shots to 14.) Barcelona made 600 passes to Juventus's 377, but Juventus
pressed more: 144 pressures to Barcelona's 122.

Messi did not score in Berlin. But no Barcelona player was on the ball more: 290 on-ball actions and
71 passes. Andrés Iniesta was Man of the Match, and it was Xavi's 767th and final appearance for the club.

## What actually changed

Put the three forwards side by side and the shift is plain:

- **Neymar:** 3 goals in the group, 7 in the knockouts.
- **Suárez:** 2 in the group, 5 in the knockouts.
- **Messi:** 8 in the group, 2 in the knockouts.

Same three players, similar totals. The goals simply moved. In the knockouts, Neymar and Suárez scored
12 of Barcelona's 16. Messi, meanwhile, finished as the competition's top assister with six, and still
ended level on ten goals with Neymar and Cristiano Ronaldo at the top of the scoring chart.

[IMAGE: blog-images/3-scorer-split.png]
*Group stage against knockouts for the three forwards.*

He had not gone missing. He had moved, from the end of the attack to the middle of it, and Barcelona
became harder to stop.

## The verdict

Thirteen games, eleven wins, two defeats (both away, both 3-2). Thirty-one goals scored, eleven conceded.
Barcelona won every home game. It was their second treble, and they became the first club to win the
treble twice.

Now go back to that phrase. A transitional year. Luis Enrique won the treble in it. Ten years later he
won the Champions League again, with Paris Saint-Germain in 2025, and then again in 2026.

That is where this series ends. This is where it starts. Next time: 2015/16.
